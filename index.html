Key idea

One latent interface for multiple skills

FIBER represents independently acquired interaction experts in the shared executable latent space of a frozen BFM and jointly learns their execution with one perceptive planner.

01

Unified representation and learning framework

FIBER represents independently acquired interaction experts in a shared executable latent space of a frozen BFM and jointly learns their execution with a unified perceptive planner.

02

Action-aligned latent policy projection

LPP identifies BFM latents by matching their induced actions to an expert's local state-dependent control responses, integrating heterogeneous experts without a unified acquisition procedure.

03

Adaptive-horizon BFM and SF-guided acquisition

Multi-γ forward-backward learning yields AH-BFM, balancing precise local control with temporally extended behavioral structure, while successor-feature-guided acquisition reuses interaction-preserving behaviors.

Method

From experts to one offline planner

Experts are projected independently, collected in a shared executable representation, and merged into one perception-conditioned latent planning dataset.

A

Heterogeneous expert acquisition

Interaction experts come from motion imitation, task-specific reward shaping, or successor-feature-guided reuse of native whole-body behaviors.

B

Action-aligned LPP

LPP identifies latent commands through the expert actions they induce over a local horizon, with behavioral anchoring reducing ambiguity among action-equivalent latents.

C

Unified latent flow planning

A DiT flow planner predicts future latent trajectories from onboard depth, proprioception, and steering tokens such as language commands or navigation waypoints.

Two feedback timescales. The planner updates lower-rate latent trajectories from perception, while the frozen BFM maps each latent and live robot state to high-rate actions.

Paper

Abstract

Learning a single perceptive policy for diverse humanoid loco-manipulation requires unifying high-level task decisions without losing the state-dependent physical responses that make each interaction succeed. Existing approaches either abstract physical realization through kinematic or contact targets, rely on offline demonstrations with limited closed-loop coverage, or require on-policy multi-expert distillation that tightly couples skill acquisition and integration. We propose FIBER, a framework that represents heterogeneous interaction experts in the executable latent space of a frozen Behavior Foundation Model (BFM). Our Latent Policy Projection (LPP) identifies latent commands whose induced actions match each expert's local control responses, enabling lower-rate planning while retaining the BFM's high-frequency state feedback. After projection, we aggregate closed-loop latent rollouts from all skills and train a single perceptive flow planner entirely offline. We further introduce AH-BFM, an adaptive-horizon BFM trained with multi-γ forward-backward learning to support both precise control and temporally extended whole-body behavior. We evaluate FIBER on box pickup, door opening, and object grasping in simulation and on a real Unitree G1 humanoid. Across 128 diverse variations per task in simulation, FIBER achieves 42.0% mean success, more than tripling projected latent replay (11.6%) and substantially outperforming kinematic planning (3.5%). Real-world experiments further demonstrate sim-to-real transfer, skill chaining, and conditional steering through the same unified planner.
